Renewed Hope for a Brighter Future

Success Stories1 February 2024

Philanthropist Joyce Frankfort has put student Alphonso Fawundu back in school after his family could no longer afford his fees.

Alphonso had already missed part of the term when his family told us they could not cover the balance. Like many households in Caldwell, the family’s income depends on daily work, and a short illness or a slow month is all it takes to put school out of reach.

A knock on the gate, and a place kept open

Ms. Frankfort, who has supported children at Rosetta Steps before, stepped in when she heard about the case. Her gift settled the outstanding balance and covered the rest of the school year, so Alphonso returned to his class with his uniform, books and seat exactly as he had left them.

For the school, the moment mattered beyond one child. When a family falls behind, the hardest part is often the shame of coming back. Keeping a place open — and making the return ordinary — is part of what a community school is for.

  • Outstanding fees cleared for the school year
  • Uniform and learning materials replaced
  • Reading-room support restarted after class

What it took

Alphonso is back in class, and his teachers report that he picked up where he left off. His family now knows that a bad month does not have to mean the end of a school year — and other families have come forward to ask how sponsorship works.

Gifts like Ms. Frankfort’s are how a school of 175 children stays open in a country where most families pay fees out of weekly earnings. They are also how a single missed term stops turning into a lost education.
