Teaching & learning

Academic programmes from first steps to Grade 6

Schooling for children from preschool to Grade 6, taught in English and rooted in the Revised Liberian National Curriculum, with the individual attention of a small school.

Our approach

High expectations, generously supported

Our classes are all taught in English. Most of our students come from families where more than half of the parents are unlettered, and where electricity, computers and televisions are not available to support learning.

We meet that reality by adapting international-standard reading and writing materials to the context of our students' lives, and by equipping our teachers with everything they need to build a good classroom environment. Lessons are interactive and fun, with a strong emphasis on retention.

English-medium Liberian national curriculum Common Core standards “I Do, We Do, You Do”

The programmes

Three stages, one continuous journey

Children move through our school with the same teachers watching their progress — so nothing gets lost between one grade and the next.

Programme

Preschool

18 months – 3 years · ABC1 & ABC2

Our preschool programme is balanced between academic and social skills. A dynamic team of four preschool teachers, headed by Johnetta Anderson, makes sure that learning is fun, creative and exciting — and sets young learners up for success in the classes ahead.

Letters & sounds

Recognising, reciting and writing the alphabet, learning letter sounds and speaking clearly.

Numbers & pencil control

Counting, number recognition, tracing lines and holding a pencil or crayon correctly.

Social skills

Sorting, sharing, playing with others, following rules and naming feelings.

Focus & routines

Sitting with an activity for fifteen minutes or more, and learning the rhythm of a school day.

By the end of preschool, children recognise and write letters and numbers, hold a pencil properly, follow instructions and stay engaged in an activity for fifteen minutes or more.

Programme

Kindergarten

K1 & K2

K1 and K2 are a time of growth, expansion and preparation for the adventure that begins in grade school. Our teachers blend play, singing and craft making with introductions to more advanced academic skills.

Basic Mathematics

Counting, number sense, simple addition and subtraction, shapes and measurement.

Language Arts

Listening, speaking, storytelling and early writing through daily shared reading.

Phonemic Awareness

Hearing, blending and separating the sounds inside words — the engine of early reading.

Science & Exploration

Weather, plants, animals and simple experiments that teach children to ask “why?”

Social Studies

Family, community, Liberian culture and the wider world around them.

Vocabulary Development

New words every week, used in conversation, song and play.

K2 learners complete a reading readiness check before moving into Grade 1, so that support starts on the first day rather than the first report card.

Programme

Grade School

Grades 1 – 6

Our grade school curriculum combines the Revised Liberian National Curriculum with the Common Core State Standards developed in the United States. Students meet every national requirement while being exposed to high-quality academic programming in mathematics, English, language arts and literacy. Learning goals set out clearly what a child should know and be able to do by the end of each school year.

Commerce Computer Science English Handwriting Home Economics Mathematics Phonics Quantitative Aptitude Reading Comprehension Science Sight Words Social Studies Spelling Verbal Aptitude

Grade 6 learners sit the national examination and receive transition support into secondary school, including scholarship guidance.

The school day

Hours, breaks and what it costs

A full day of learning

  • Monday to Thursday — 8:30am to 4:00pm

  • Morning break — 11:45am to 12:30pm

Less than a dollar a day

The average cost of educating one child for a school year is USD $280. Some of our families are able to pay this cost. Most are not.

That is why scholarships sit at the centre of everything we do, and why a gift of $280 can keep a child in a classroom for an entire year.

How we teach

Five things you will notice in our classrooms

Reading is scheduled, not squeezed in

Every class has a protected reading block. Literacy is the spine of the timetable, not an afterthought.

Foundations before speed

The “I Do, We Do, You Do” model: we show it, we do it together, then the child does it alone.

Learning is active

Children build, measure, act, sing and present. Fewer copied notes; more thinking out loud.

Every child is known

Teachers track progress and wellbeing together, and flag concerns early — school is a safe place here.

Teachers keep learning

Training, coaching and peer observation — because teacher quality is the biggest in-school factor in a child's success.

Families are partners

Regular conversations with parents, home reading routines and an open office door.
