About Rosetta Steps

It takes a community to raise a community

That has been our motto since 2014, when one reading room in Duala became a school — and a neighbourhood began to believe in its children.

Our story

From one reading room to a school of 175

Rosetta Steps Education Center was founded in 2014 by Ama Harris. Parents in the Duala community were desperate for help: their children were not learning to read, and there was nowhere affordable to turn. Seeing the need, Ama transformed her mother's childhood home into the organisation's base.

A reading room opened in 2015, equipped with books, computers, televisions and other learning aids. The school formally opened in 2016 and has continued to grow in size, scope, quality and impact.

In 2020, in response to demand, we moved into a purpose-built campus that can hold 175 students, with seven classrooms, a science lab, a library and staff offices. Families now walk to us from Caldwell, Duala and the surrounding communities. Some pay full fees, many learn on scholarship, and all share the same classrooms, the same books and the same high expectations.

Milestones

Twelve years, one steady step at a time

We add a grade only when we can staff it well, and we open a programme only when the community asks for it. That is why the growth has been slow, deliberate and lasting.

  • 2014

    The centre is founded

    Ama Harris opens Rosetta Steps in her mother's childhood home in Duala, named for educator Rosetta Benson Harris.

  • 2015

    The reading room opens

    Books, computers, televisions and learning aids arrive. Children from the neighbourhood begin to read after school.

  • 2016

    The school opens its doors

    Rosetta Steps becomes a formal school, from preschool upward, teaching in English at a cost families can reach.

  • 2020

    A purpose-built campus

    Seven classrooms, a science lab, a library and staff offices — room for 175 students and the programmes that surround them.

Our Mission

To empower and educate as many Liberian youth and families in need as we can. We are founded on the belief that education is a human right and the best means of combatting inequality, poverty and discrimination.

  • Teach every child to read fluently and with understanding.
  • Keep quality education affordable for low-income families.
  • Support teachers with training, materials and respect.

Our Vision

A Liberia where the potential for greatness in every child is recognised, nurtured and given room to grow — and where education is never rationed by family income.

  • Literacy as a right, not a privilege.
  • Schools that anchor their communities.
  • A generation of confident Liberian leaders.

Core values

Six commitments we keep every day

These are not posters on a wall. They are the questions we ask ourselves when we plan a lesson, hire a teacher or welcome a new family.

Education for All

Every child in Liberia, whatever their social or economic status, deserves an education that sets them up for success in life.

High expectations

We adapt international-standard reading and writing materials to our context — and then we expect every learner to meet them.

Community

Teachers, parents, administrators, volunteers, donors and mentors all pull in the same direction: the child.

Integrity

We are transparent with families, donors and partners, and we keep our promises.

Compassion

We support our students' mental and emotional health, and we make school a safe place where every child feels known and valued.

Lifelong learning

Children, teachers and parents keep learning. A school should never stop growing.

Education for All

Access is the beginning, not the finish line

More than half of our students' parents are unlettered, and most homes have no electricity, computer or television to support learning. Many of our students would otherwise have been kept at home to carry out chores or child labour.

Our answer is not to lower the bar. It is to remove every obstacle in front of it:

  • English-medium teaching with reading and writing materials adapted to the lives of our students.
  • Well-equipped teachers with the resources to build a good classroom environment.
  • Interactive lessons built on the “I Do, We Do, You Do” model of foundational learning.
  • Scholarships and fee support so that roughly $280 a school year never decides a child's future.
  • A safe, inclusive campus where every child is known by name.

Leadership

Ama Harris

Founder & Executive Director

Ama Harris is the daughter of Rosetta Benson and a Liberian committed to community development and education. In 2014 she turned her mother's childhood home into the base for Rosetta Steps — and has led its growth ever since.

Alongside the school, Ama is an executive in the travel and tourism industry and the owner of Xquisite Liberia. She holds a Bachelor of Business Administration in Management from Stella Maris Polytechnic University (Arthur Barclay Business College) and a Travel and Tourism Consultant Diploma with the International Air Transport Association.

A French and English speaker and a proud member of the Rotary Club of Liberia, Ama's leadership is the driving force behind the growth and impact of Rosetta Steps Education Center.

Our namesake

Rosetta Benson Harris

Rosetta Steps is named for Ama's mother, a revered Liberian educator and former principal of the William V. S. Tubman High School. Her standard — that every child can learn to read, and that no family should be turned away — is the one we still hold ourselves to.

Her family and former students remain part of the school's life, joining celebrations and reading with our youngest learners.
